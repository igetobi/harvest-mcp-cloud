1. The idea in one paragraph

One line, one question, nothing else. We email a plumbing contractor and ask whether they do a specific high-ticket job in their city. It does not read like a cold email, so more of them answer. When they answer, we start the real conversation: we can send them leads for that exact job, not general plumbing calls.

2. The jobs, and which ones are worth split testing

Your note on whole house wiring applies cleanly to plumbing. Some jobs every plumber does, so asking tells us nothing. The table separates the two.

VariableJobWhat a contractor chargesIn the split test?
job1sewer line replacements$4,000 to $15,000Yes, lead question
job2water main replacements$1,600 to $5,000Yes, pairs with job1
job3trenchless sewer line replacementstop half of the sewer rangeYes, the sharpest filter
job4whole-home repipes$4,000 to $20,000Reference only
job5tankless water heater installs$3,000 to $12,500No, every plumber does these

job4 and job5 are in the table for reference, not for the test. Nearly every plumber repipes a house and installs tankless heaters, so the answer is always yes and we learn nothing. That is the same reason you pulled whole house wiring out of the electrical set.

job1 and job2 are the pair. Sewer lines and water mains are both underground excavation work. A plumber who does one does the other, so they belong in a single question, the way EV chargers and generators do for electricians.

job3 is the sharpest filter we have. Trenchless needs equipment most plumbers do not own, so a yes tells us we are talking to a serious operator with the highest-value jobs on the truck. It is the obvious thing to split test the pair against.

5. How the split test runs

Two versions of Step 1, everything after it identical, so any difference in replies is the question itself.

  • Version A, the pair (more inboxes): "Do you do sewer line & water main replacements in {{city}}?"
  • Version B, the filter (fewer inboxes): "Do you do trenchless sewer line replacements in {{city}}?"

As you asked on the electrical campaign, the version you favour carries the larger share of the inboxes and the challenger runs on fewer. Steps 2, 3 and 4 do not change between versions.

One thing to set up before we send

{{city}} is new. Our Instantly campaigns currently carry {{location}}, not {{city}}, so the column has to be added on the upload or the first line of every email goes out broken. The Apollo export already has a City column, so it is a mapping job rather than new research.

The variable also has to be spelled {{city}} with no space. A space breaks the merge tag in Instantly and the email sends with the raw text showing.
