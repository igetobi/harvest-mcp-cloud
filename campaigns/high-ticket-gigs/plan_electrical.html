1. The idea in one paragraph

One line, one question, nothing else. We email an electrical contractor and ask whether they do a specific high-ticket job. It does not read like a cold email, so more of them answer. When they answer, we start the real conversation: we can send them leads for that exact job, not general electrical calls.

This is the simple version you asked for on the 4 November call. Tobi is building the longer, more detailed copy separately, so this runs alongside it rather than instead of it.

2. The five jobs we are testing

Each is a real high-ticket job, in the words an electrician actually uses. Prices are 2026 US averages, and they are here to show why these five and not others.

job5200 amp service upgrades$1,500 to $6,500Yes, nearly all

Why these five and not the biggest-ticket jobs on earth: the whole thing only works if the honest answer is yes. A job that pays huge but almost nobody does gets us "no, we don't do that", and the conversation is over before it starts. So every job above is big money and widely offered.

On job1 and job5: that is the same job twice, in two different sets of words. How a homeowner says it, and how an electrician says it. It is the cheapest thing we can learn in this whole test, so it is worth one of the five slots.

3. The emails

Day 0, Step 1: the question

Subject: do you do {{job1}}?

Hi {{firstName}},

Do you do {{job1}}?

{{sendingAccountFirstName}}

That is the entire email. No company name, no pitch, no link.

Day 2, Step 2: the nudge

Same thread, no subject line.

Hi {{firstName}},

Just making sure this reached you. Do you take these on?

{{sendingAccountFirstName}}

One human nudge. A real person chasing a quote sends exactly one.

Day 5, Step 3: the reveal and the offer

Same thread, no subject line.

{{firstName}},

I should be straight with you about why I asked.

I run lead generation for electrical contractors, and that is the job where our leads are worth the most to the contractor. We work pay per sale. No ad spend, no retainer, no setup fee. You pay when the job closes.

We take one company per service area and we do not have anyone in {{location}}.

Would you be against a 15 minute call to see what we could send you?

{{sendingAccountFirstName}} from Black Swan Media

Day 9, Step 4: close out

Same thread, no subject line.

{{firstName}},

Should we postpone this to early next quarter? Let me know either way and I will stop following up.

Thanks,

{{sendingAccountFirstName}} from Black Swan Media

4. When someone replies "yes we do those"

This is the most important message in the campaign, and a human sends it, not the sequence. It is the pivot you described on the call.

{{firstName}},

Good, that is what I was hoping.

To be upfront: I am not looking to have one done. I run lead generation for electrical contractors, and we can send you leads for that specific job rather than general service calls.

It is pay per sale, so there is no ad spend and nothing up front. We work with one company per service area and we do not have a partner in {{location}} yet.

Does Monday or Tuesday afternoon work for 15 minutes?

{{sendingAccountFirstName}} from Black Swan Media

Whoever is on the inbox needs this saved as a snippet before we turn the campaign on, or the replies go cold while we write it.

5. How the test runs in Instantly

There are two ways to do this. We recommend the first.

Option A: five variants of Step 1 (recommended)

Build Step 1 with five variants, each hardcoding one job. Instantly rotates them evenly and, with auto variant select set to reply rate, promotes the winner on its own. That setting is already switched on in our Pest Control and Electrical campaigns, so there is nothing new to configure.

  • Every lead gets the same sequence, and only the job in Step 1 changes
  • Reply rate per variant tells us which job pulls, directly
  • No extra columns on the lead upload
  • One campaign to watch instead of five

Option B: one email with a job variable per lead

Upload the leads with a custom column holding the job, and Step 1 merges it in. This splits the list five ways instead of rotating variants.

Use this if we later want to match the job to the lead, for example sending generator installs only to contractors in storm states. For a straight read on which job pulls best, Option A is cleaner.

One technical note

If we go the variable route, the variable has to be {{job1}}, with no space. {{job 1}} will not merge, because a space breaks the merge tag in Instantly, and the email would go out with the raw text showing or the line blank. Small thing, but it would ruin a send.
